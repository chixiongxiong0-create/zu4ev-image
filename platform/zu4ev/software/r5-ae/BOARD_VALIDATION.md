# Pending board validation

No hardware was available for this revision. The following checks are required before treating the AE path as operational.

Offline evidence, 2026-10-27: the FE event test passed pending/acknowledgment, coincident FE and acknowledgment, timestamps, and 16-bit FE and 14-bit AXI SOF Gray counter wrap. The host AE tests passed exposure/gain priority, deadband and an N+2 delayed plant. The R5 ELF linked wholly into ATCM/BTCM. The complete Vivado build passed with final setup WNS 0.055 ns and hold WHS 0.010 ns; these margins are small. Reports are under `build/ae_r5/reports/20261027-091407-45188`. Bootgen produced the matching RAM test image under `build/ae_r5/ram_boot/BOOT.BIN`; it has not been booted on the board.

1. Boot the matching FSBL, bitstream and R5 ELF using `tools/ram_boot_ltm.tcl` and a DDR-staged boot image. Capture FSBL UART output and confirm sensor ID, standby register readback and stream startup. Keep the existing QSPI contents and backup intact.
2. Check that R5 starts, `ae_log.magic` is `0x41453552`, `fault` remains zero, FE and ISP sequences advance, and `dropped_fe` does not grow. Obtain `ae_log` and `ae_records` addresses from the exact ELF with `armr5-none-eabi-nm`; they may change at each link. Use `decode_log.py` on the binary dumps described by its input filenames.
3. Capture SCL and SDA at the physical IOBUF outputs with ILA or an external analyzer. Measure frequency and high/low times and confirm sensor ACK and register readback at 400 kHz. Confirm pullup rise time and level compliance at the sensor pins, preferably with an oscilloscope.
4. Record FE to AXI video SOF, IRQ to first IIC write, completion of both exposure writes, and frame period. Measure the CSI FS short packet separately: the current SOF counter marks the first AXI output pixel. Establish the actual sensor latch boundary and that every high/low byte pair finishes safely before it. Check FE and AXI SOF counters across wrap and while Linux runs.
5. Change scene illumination in controlled steps. Capture per-frame mean, exposure and gain, identify which actual sensor frames reflect each write, verify the expected N+2 behavior and demonstrate convergence and stability in both directions. Include the darkest and brightest conditions and record fault or saturation cases.

The previous design's board report measured approximately 120 frames/s with HTS 780 and VTS 529. At a nominal 400 kHz, one IIC write with a 7-bit device address, two register address bytes and one data byte consumes at least 36 SCL periods, about 90 µs excluding START/STOP, bus free time, software and clock stretching. Two separate exposure writes therefore need at least 180 µs against a nominal 8.33 ms frame. This is a budget estimate; actual timings and the usable sensor update window remain to be measured.

Group hold is not confirmed in the supplied SC130GS documentation. A second-byte error or a frame crossing can leave a partial update; the R5 latches a fault but cannot guarantee atomic rollback. Do not infer group hold support from the software behavior.

## Board run, 2026-10-27

A temporary JTAG/DDR boot was dispatched with `build/ae_r5/ram_boot/BOOT.BIN` and `build/ae_stats/qspi/image.ub`; QSPI was not programmed. The FSBL UART log reported `SENSOR_MODE_OK`, `SENSOR_STANDBY_VERIFY_OK`, `PIPELINE_RX_OK`, `PIPELINE_CAPTURE_OK` and `SENSOR_READY`. R5 started, initialized its IIC controller and read the initial sensor configuration, but `ae_log` reported fault 9, zero FE interrupts and zero AE records. Its configured IIC clock value was 378787 Hz, not a physical waveform measurement. GPIO bank 5 showed a changing AXI SOF Gray count and an FE count fixed at zero.

The synthesized CSI RX controller netlist `build/ae_r5/camera/camera.cache/ip/2025.1/c/9/c91605fa75e64d45/bd_a423_rx_0_sim_netlist.v` assigns `frame_rcvd_pulse_out` to constant zero. Thus the FE pulse connected to `ae_fe_event` cannot fire in this configuration. PG232 states this port is available when the CSI-2 controller register interface is disabled; the current design enables that interface for `csi_control`. The controller's interrupt-status register bit 31 indicates FE and is write-one-to-clear; a redesigned and verified interrupt/acknowledgment path is required before retrying AE. Its timing, clearing races, diagnostic ownership, and clock-domain crossing require verification. AXI SOF is not a substitute for sensor FE.

The board run did not measure IIC waveforms, exposure write timing, sensor N+2 behavior, or AE convergence. Raw boot and UART logs are `build/ae_r5/board_boot.log` and `build/ae_r5/board_uart.log`.

## FE register interrupt board run, 2026-10-27

PL uses the CSI controller's FE status/interrupt and W1C, then forwards the notification with a four-phase clock-domain handshake. The R5 firmware retains an interrupt acknowledge timeout fault (11). The XSim controller test passed initialization, two FE notifications and suppression of an event after a failed W1C response; the existing FE latch/Gray-wrap test also passed. These tests do not establish exhaustive reset, concurrency or physical-frame coverage.

The complete design build passed DRC and the scripted timing gate: setup WNS 0.106 ns, hold WHS 0.010 ns; see `build/ae_r5/reports/20261027-215448-10716/timing.rpt`. The RAM-only boot in `build/ae_r5/ram_boot_fe` was loaded via JTAG; QSPI was not programmed. FSBL confirmed sensor setup and streaming. With Linux running, JTAG snapshots over ten seconds showed 1,203 FE interrupts (120.3/s), fault zero, and dropped FE count steady at 2. After a large R5 TCM dump the count increased from 2 to 8, then remained at 8 for a subsequent ten seconds with 1,204 interrupts. The temporal association with the dump does not establish its cause.

`build/ae_r5/board_fe_dump/decoded.json` contains 512 sampled records, mean approximately 95, and zero exposure or gain changes in that interval because the scene was within the deadband. The debugger rejected the BTCM read without `mrd -force`; the force read returned plausible records but its impact on real-time operation and the snapshot's coherence remain unverified. The dump was captured while the ring was changing. No physical SCL measurement, FE packet timing, N+2 transition, controlled light-step convergence, or group hold proof was obtained. Sticky ISR.FE can coalesce physical frames before clear, and the reported FE timestamp includes AXI service and CDC latency. The current FE count and timestamp cannot prove that a sensor register write ended before the physical FE or latch boundary.
